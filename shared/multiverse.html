The Multiverse

Many worlds. One question: which one are you in?

The multiverse is the idea that our universe is not alone — that reality may contain many universes, each with its own history, laws, or starting conditions.

Ways people mean it

  • Cosmic / inflationary — bubble universes from eternal inflation, each cooling into its own cosmos.
  • Quantum (many-worlds) — every quantum outcome branches into a parallel history.
  • Landscape — string theory’s huge space of possible vacuum states, each a different physics.
  • Simulation / fiction — stacked realities in stories, games, and thought experiments.

Why it fascinates

It turns “why is the world like this?” into “among all possible worlds, why this one?” Fine-tuning, free will, identity, and luck all look different if countless versions of you exist — or none of you do.

What we can say carefully

Some multiverse ideas grow out of serious physics; others are philosophy or metaphor. We have not visited another universe. What we have are models that make the idea hard to dismiss — and stories that make it hard to forget.
